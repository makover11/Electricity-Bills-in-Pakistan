What Are Fixed Charges on Electricity Bills in Pakistan?

Electricity bills in Pakistan consist of several components. Besides the energy charge (which depends on how many units you consume), there are fixed charges that customers must pay regardless of their usage. Fixed charges often include items like meter rent, customer-service fees, and capacity or connection charges. These are added to the bill on a monthly basis or per billing cycle, even if you use no electricity.

In the past, fixed charges were mainly applied to commercial or industrial customers, with residential customers having little or no fixed fees. However, as of early 2026, the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (NEPRA) introduced a new tariff structure that applies mandatory fixed charges to most domestic consumers. This guide explains what fixed charges are, how they are calculated in Pakistan, and how they differ from other bill components.

Electricity Bill Components

Most Pakistani power bills are broken down into these major parts:

  • Energy Charges: The per-unit cost for electricity you actually consume (measured in kilowatt-hours, kWh). For example, if you use 100 units at Rs20/kWh, the energy charge is Rs2,000.
  • Fixed Charges: Flat fees or capacity-based charges that do not depend on actual consumption. This can include meter rent, customer-service fees, and capacity charges. These apply even if consumption is zero.
  • Fuel Price Adjustments (FPA/QTA): Surcharges that adjust for changes in fuel (oil, gas) costs. These vary monthly or quarterly and are added on top of the basic rates.
  • Taxes and Duties: Government taxes like GST on electricity duty or distribution charges. These are usually percentage charges applied to portions of the bill, not fixed amounts.
  • Arrears or Previous Balance: Any unpaid amount from previous bills will also show up. These are not the same as fixed charges on a new bill.

In this article we focus on the Fixed Charges component, explaining why they are charged and how to calculate them.

What Are Fixed Charges?

Fixed charges on an electricity bill are fees that do not depend on how much power you use. They typically cover the fixed costs of maintaining your connection – for example, the cost of distributing power to your house, meter maintenance, or customer services. For commercial and industrial customers, Pakistan’s tariffs have long included fixed-capacity charges (based on sanctioned load or maximum demand). However, for residential consumers these were minimal or zero until recent tariff reforms.

Starting in early 2026, NEPRA’s tariff rationalization means nearly all domestic consumers now have a fixed monthly charge linked to their sanctioned load. Under this new system, households pay a fixed fee per kilowatt of their contracted capacity (sanctioned load). For example, if the fixed-charge rate is Rs 300/kW and your sanctioned load is 5 kW, you pay Rs 1,500 every month as a fixed charge, even if your consumption is very low. NEPRA set these residential fixed charges in a range from about Rs200/kW/month up to Rs675/kW/month, depending on consumer category. (Previously, “lifeline” consumers below a certain low-usage threshold were exempt from fixed charges.)

Notably, K-Electric (the Karachi utility) follows the uniform tariff policy but uses a slightly different approach. Under its schedule, domestic consumers below 700 units pay no fixed charge, and above that there are block flat fixed charges (e.g. Rs200 for 701–800 units, Rs400 for 801–1000 units, etc). This contrasts with other DISCOs where fixed charges are purely based on sanctioned load. For most of Pakistan (LESCO, IESCO, PESCO, etc.), the fixed charge is capacity-based as per NEPRA’s approved tariff.

Sanctioned Load and Billing Basis

Your sanctioned load is the maximum power capacity approved for your connection (usually in kilowatts, kW). For example, a typical household might have a sanctioned load of 3 kW or 5 kW. This is listed on your power bill or in your connection paperwork. Since NEPRA’s 2026 tariff change, the fixed charge for residential consumers is calculated by multiplying a fixed rate (Rs per kW) by your sanctioned load.

For industrial or time-of-use (ToU) customers, billing may use the Maximum Demand Indicator (MDI) – a meter reading that records the highest power draw in kW over a period. But for almost all domestic categories under the new rules, the charge is simply (rate × sanctioned load). That means even if you consume very little electricity, the capacity you have contracted still carries a cost.

Calculating Fixed Charges (Example)

To see how fixed charges work in practice, consider this example. Suppose the fixed charge rate is Rs 500 per kW per month (Rs500/kW). If your sanctioned load is 6 kW, then your fixed charge is:

DescriptionAmount
Sanctioned Load6 kW
Fixed Charge RateRs 500 per kW
Fixed Charge (monthly)Rs 3,000
Energy Usage250 kWh
Energy Charge RateRs 20 per kWh
Energy Charge (250 × Rs20)Rs 5,000
Total (Fixed + Energy)Rs 8,000

This illustrates that even with moderate consumption (250 units), the fixed portion (Rs3,000) can be a large share of your bill. If you consume no units at all in a month, you would still pay Rs3,000 in this example (plus any taxes or adjustments).

Who Pays Fixed Charges?

As of the current tariff, almost all domestic categories pay fixed charges based on sanctioned load, except the very lowest "lifeline" slab. Lifeline consumers (usually up to 100 units) continue to have no fixed charge to protect low-income households. Consumers in higher consumption slabs (even those previously “protected” up to 300 units) now face fixed charges from Rs200/kW upward. Commercial and industrial customers have always paid fixed charges – often based on sanctioned load or maximum demand. For example, a small shop might pay a flat meter rent (e.g. Rs 300/month) plus any capacity-based charge if applicable.

Taxes, Adjustments, and Other Charges

Fixed charges are distinct from other bill components such as taxes and fuel adjustments. Pakistan imposes a GST (18%) on certain bill items (like electricity duty). Fuel Price Adjustment (FPA) or Quarterly Tariff Adjustment (QTA) charges vary with fuel costs and are calculated per unit consumed, not as fixed fees. Similarly, K-Electric’s monthly Fuel Surcharge (DSS) is usage-based. These all fluctuate with usage or policy. Fixed charges simply add to the base bill independently of kWh consumed.

Finding Fixed Charges on Your Bill

To verify fixed charges on your actual bill, look for sections labeled “Fixed Charge” or “Capacity Charge” or “Meter Rent.” It should show your sanctioned load (kW) and the applicable rate (Rs/kW), with the product as a line item. If you have a new digital or itemized bill, fixed charges may be included under “Customer Service Charges” or similar. Compare this number against (sanctioned load × rate) to ensure it is correct. If anything seems off, consult your DISCO’s published tariff schedule or contact customer support. K-Electric customers should refer to their uniform tariff schedule or the online portal.

How to Check Your Sanctioned Load

Your sanctioned load (capacity) is usually given in your connection documents or printed on the electricity meter (e.g., “3.5 kW” or “5.0 kW”). You can also find it on your last bill under the tariff details. If unsure, contact your local DISCO (e.g. LESCO, MEPCO, etc.) to confirm the authorized load for your connection.

FAQs: Fixed Charges

What exactly are fixed charges?

Fixed charges cover the costs of maintaining your power connection (lines, meters, billing). You pay them whether or not you use electricity. If you use no electricity in a month, you would still pay the fixed-charge portion (except lifeline customers, who are exempt).

How is my fixed charge calculated?

It's typically (Sanctioned Load in kW) × (Fixed Charge rate in Rs/kW). For example, 4 kW × Rs300/kW = Rs1,200 per month. Some older schemes use a flat meter rent instead.

Are small (lifeline) consumers exempt?

Yes. Lifeline consumers (usually 1-100 units per month) currently pay no fixed charge to help low-income households. All higher domestic slabs have fixed charges applied.

Do all DISCOs charge fixed fees the same way?

Most DISCOs (LESCO, IESCO, etc.) follow NEPRA’s per-kW model. K-Electric uses its own schedule (flat fees above 700 units). Always check your specific DISCO’s published tariff.

What if I use very little electricity?

You’ll still pay the fixed charge based on your full sanctioned load. This is because fixed charges reflect the capacity reserved for you, regardless of actual usage.

Can I reduce my fixed charges?

To lower fixed charges, you would need a lower sanctioned load. In practice, that means requesting a smaller connection capacity from your DISCO (if possible). Before doing that, use the calculator above to see how your bill changes.

Do fixed charges change monthly?

Fixed charges remain constant month-to-month unless your sanctioned load or the tariff rate changes (for example, at a tariff revision). They are not tied to your monthly consumption.
